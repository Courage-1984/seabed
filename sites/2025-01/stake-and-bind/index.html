01 / The Antidote

Hedge-laying is the opposite of your job

A hedge is a living thing. It doesn't ship in sprints. It doesn't have a roadmap or a quarterly OKR. It just grows, slowly, stubbornly, and if you treat it right, it'll outlive your company. Hedge-laying is the craft of cutting partially through the stems of shrubs — hazel, blackthorn, hawthorn — and bending them down horizontally, weaving them between vertical stakes, then binding the top with flexible withies. The result is a dense, stock-proof barrier that regenerates each year. It's physical work. It's precise work. And it's completely, mercifully finite. You can see what you've done by lunchtime. There's no stand-up about whether the hedge is aligned with company values. There's just the hedge, and your hands, and the slow satisfaction of something that's actually finished.

By Sunday, you'll have laid 30 metres of hedge and forgotten your password

Under the supervision of Maren Ashworth and our Master Hedgers, you learn authentic Midland-style hedge-laying across the River Leach valley. We begin with botanical identification, distinguishing hazel shoots, blackthorn stems, and hawthorn trunks before assessing sap flow rates. You master the geometry of pleaching: driving a billhook diagonally through eighty percent of a live trunk without severing the delicate cambium bark layer that keeps the stem alive.

You practice weaving horizontal pleachers around freshly driven ash stakes at sixty-degree angles, binding flexible willow withies across the top crown to create a stock-proof barrier. This work demands focus and dorsal muscles you forgot existed, but every necessary tool, thick leather apron, and visor awaits in our equipment room. You leave with genuine craftsmanship and callused thumbs.

07 / Common Queries

Questions you're probably thinking

Still got questions? →

What fitness level is required for hedge-laying?

You do not require marathon endurance, but hedge-laying involves steady physical exertion over six hours in field weather. If you enjoy a two-hour country ramble without distress, your fitness is entirely sufficient.

What happens if it rains all weekend?

Late autumn in the Cotswolds guarantees intermittent drizzle and mud. We equip you with waxed outerwear, thermal gloves, and gaiters, followed immediately by roaring hearth fires indoors.

Do I need prior countryside experience?

None whatsoever. Nearly all visiting professionals arrive having never held a billhook. Maren Ashworth assumes zero practical background, teaching tool mechanics and safety geometry from scratch before anyone touches a live branch.

What specific items do I need to pack?

We supply all forged cutting tools, aprons, and safety glasses on arrival. You only need waterproof ankle boots, thick socks, durable field trousers, thermal layers, and a reading book.

Will I feel awkward travelling alone?

Over ninety percent of attendees arrive alone, escaping digital notifications on their own terms. Groups are limited to eight peers in tech leadership. Conversation flows easily without professional posturing.
