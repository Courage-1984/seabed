01 // THE CLIFF-EDGE FURNACE AND THE SEA WIND

Our glass studio stands sixty metres above the Atlantic surge on the granite cliffs of St. Agnes Head. Most industrial glassmakers work in sealed, climate-controlled factories to prevent thermal shock from disturbing the molten gathers. We deliberately opened our western furnace wall to the prevailing ocean weather, allowing the salt air and sudden pressure drops of passing squalls to shape our glass as it leaves the pipe.

When a gather of molten silica and kelp ash is swung into an incoming gale, the windward surface cools seconds faster than the leeward core. This elemental temperature gradient freezes subtle tidal striations into the crystal wall, capturing the movement of the sea before the piece enters our lehr annealing oven for a forty-eight-hour slow cool.

Every decanter and tumbler is shaped with hand-turned applewood jacks and finished with a sheared, fire-polished lip. No two pours produce identical refractions. We release only thirty complete tableware sets per lunar cycle, each holding the light and salinity of the Cornish coastline.

The 4-Stage Tidal Glassmaking Cycle

1. Crucible Melting (1,200°C)

Our process commences with the precise formulation of local Cornish silica sand, potash flux, and incinerated kelp ash. We fire our central ceramic crucible to twelve hundred degrees Celsius, patiently coaxing the raw, opaque minerals into a pool of flawless, clear molten crystal over thirty-six intense hours.

2. Gale-Assisted Pipe Spinning

By throwing open the western seaward shutter, we invite high-velocity Atlantic winds directly onto the blowpipe. As the hot gather is spun, the biting cold sea air creates instantaneous thermal shock gradients, stretching the silica into natural, undulating forms that machine tooling cannot replicate.

3. Applewood Shaping & Jacking

We dictate the final silhouette not with metal moulds, but by tooling the glowing crystal against wet, charred Cornish applewood blocks. The steam generated acts as a microscopic cushion between the wood and the glass, preserving the pristine exterior surface while the artisan dictates the flare of the lip.

4. 48-Hour Lehr Annealing

To relieve the immense internal structural stresses born from such extreme elemental conflict, every vessel is transferred to our lehr annealing oven. Here, the temperature is painstakingly reduced to match the ambient coastal air over a continuous forty-eight-hour cycle, ensuring permanent structural integrity and crystalline clarity.
