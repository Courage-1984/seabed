The Digital Waveform Repository.

High-fidelity 3D acoustic matrices of catalogued prehistoric resonant minerals.

This repository provides open-access digital preservation of some of the most fragile acoustic artifacts known to archaeology. All datasets represent non-contact elastography measurements collected under stringent atmospheric isolation inside our Sterkfontein facility. We offer these undamped, high-resolution models to support global forensic organological research, allowing scholars to study ancient soundscapes without risking further physical degradation to the original archaeological specimens. The integrity of these digital matrices relies on our exact adherence to ISO/IEC 17025 accredited acoustic measurement procedures.

Physical Material & Damping Co-efficient Analysis

The precise rate of sound decay within an archaeological specimen is inextricably linked to its internal crystalline geometry. By applying rigorous geological physics to ancient sound production, we calculate exact damping coefficients based on structural mineralogy. This analytical framework moves beyond simple frequency mapping, offering a comprehensive mathematical model of how specific lithic materials convert mechanical energy into acoustic pressure over time. By isolating the internal friction metrics, we can reconstruct the original acoustic parameters of artifacts that have suffered partial structural failure.

Our empirical observations demonstrate that dense quartzites (2.65 g/cm³) exhibit a remarkably high Young's modulus, propagating high-frequency waves with minimal internal friction. This density allows for a prolonged sustain and a bright, resonant attack, making it an ideal material for early hominid percussion tools. In contrast, subterranean dolomites (2.84 g/cm³) contain microscopic porosity resulting from calcitic dissolution, which rapidly absorbs overtones and drastically lowers the Q-factor, creating a dull, rapidly decaying sound profile. Granitic structures present intermediate acoustic profiles; their complex multi-mineral matrices (quartz, feldspar, and mica) scatter internal wave fronts, resulting in shorter sustain but complex, inharmonic transients that reflect the chaotic internal boundary layers of the stone.

Published Analytical Reports

Our extraction methodologies form the basis of several peer-reviewed social proofs and have achieved broad academic validation. The Sterkfontein data sets are frequently cited across international palaeo-acoustic literature as the benchmark for non-destructive wave mapping. These publications validate the extreme measures taken by our laboratory to eliminate ambient noise and thermal drift, cementing our reputation as the premier facility for forensic organological research.

In "Acoustic Mapping of Makapansgat Lithics" (DOI: 10.1016/j.jas.2024.11.002), our team validated the resonant consistency of chert artifacts over a simulated 10,000-year burial timeline, proving that subterranean exposure does not fundamentally alter the primary modal frequencies. Lead researcher Dr. H. Venter noted, "The non-contact methodology utilized by the Sterkfontein lab allowed us to perceive the exact acoustic footprint of the artifact without risking a single micro-fracture." We also co-authored "Vibrometric Analysis of Dolomitic Speleothems" (DOI: 10.1016/j.jas.2025.04.017), which detailed the resonant modal behaviour of intact cave formations under ambient noise conditions, and "Elastographic Reconstruction of Early Hominid Soundscapes" (DOI: 10.1016/j.jas.2026.01.009), a landmark paper that mapped the entire acoustic field of the Cradle of Humankind using our mobile laser interferometry rigs.
