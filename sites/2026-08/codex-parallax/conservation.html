Non-Aqueous Conservation Protocols

Historic vellum demands extreme caution during conservation; the application of liquid water invariably disrupts the internal collagen linkages, causing permanent cockling and rigidification upon drying. To combat this, Codex Parallax Laboratory entirely eschews aqueous baths in favor of targeted, moisture-controlled molecular gels. Our clinical protocol restores absolute flexibility to the parchment without introducing synthetic plasticizers or irreversible chemical softeners that alter the manuscript's native chemistry.

This rigorous adherence to dry and semi-dry techniques guarantees that the structural integrity of the calfskin remains completely uncompromised. Archivists receive a stabilized, robust document suitable for long-term exhibition, unburdened by the dimensional warping common to legacy restoration methods.

1. Micro-Aspiration & Surface Dusting

Using ultra-fine badger-hair brushes and a highly regulated HEPA micro-aspirator, loose particulate matter, fungal spores, and unbound soot are meticulously lifted from the folium surface.

2. Gellan Gel Application

A precision-cast 4% rigid gellan-gum gel strip is placed in direct contact with the soiled area for exactly 15 minutes, allowing capillary action to draw out ingrained dirt without releasing free water.

3. Proteolytic Enzyme Surface Treatment

Micro-doses of highly specific proteolytic enzymes are applied via the gel interface to chemically digest hardened animal-glue accretions, safely dissolving binding residues without damaging the keratin.

4. Controlled Gore-Tex Chamber Drying

The treated folium is relaxed and dried within a specialized Gore-Tex humidity chamber, permitting slow, even moisture equalization that prevents cellular collapse and maintains the document's natural elasticity.

Archival Exhibition Mounting & Micro-Tension Frames

A stabilized palimpsest requires a mounting solution that respects the dynamic nature of animal skin. Standard rigid mounting severely stresses the folium during inevitable seasonal humidity fluctuations, frequently leading to disastrous tearing. Our laboratory engineers custom micro-tension enclosures designed to allow the parchment to expand and contract freely within a strictly controlled microclimate.

This floating suspension system utilizes zero adhesives on the artifact itself, ensuring complete reversibility. The final sealed module provides absolute protection against physical handling, UV radiation, and atmospheric pollutants, securing the manuscript's legacy for centuries.

  • Japanese Tissue Hinges: Long-fiber kozo paper hinges, applied with reversible methylcellulose, secure the folium edges without restricting micro-movements.
  • Anodized Aluminum Micro-Tension Clips: Precision-engineered spring clips maintain gentle, even tension across the manuscript, dynamically adjusting to hygroscopic expansion.
  • UV-Filtering Tru Vue Optium Acrylic: Glazing that blocks 99% of damaging ultraviolet radiation while providing anti-reflective optical clarity for exhibition.
  • Sealed Inert Gas Enclosures: The entire frame is hermetically sealed and purged with inert argon gas to arrest ongoing oxidative degradation of the iron-gall ink.

Archival & Technical Paleography FAQ

Does 365nm UV light radiation cause fade or damage to historic manuscript inks?

No. Our 16-band LED arrays operate at extremely low lux intensities, triggering fluorescence without generating the radiant energy necessary to break molecular bonds. The total photon exposure during a complete scan equates to less than one hour of standard museum exhibition lighting.

Can the multispectral scanner recover text from severely burned or water-damaged manuscripts?

Yes. By utilizing the 1050nm near-infrared bandwidth, the optical system can penetrate deep carbon char and heavy mold staining, frequently revealing completely legible text layers hidden beneath catastrophic physical damage.

What digital file formats are delivered to paleographers after image processing?
