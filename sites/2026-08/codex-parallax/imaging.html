Statistical Processing: Independent Component Analysis

Raw multispectral frames are visually useless until mathematically processed. Our pipeline begins with precise sub-pixel registration alignment, ensuring that all 16 frames stack perfectly despite microscopic thermal drift during the scanning session. Following alignment, we apply rigorous flat-field correction to eliminate any uneven LED illumination or lens vignetting, guaranteeing that intensity values represent true chemical fluorescence.

The registered data matrix is then subjected to Independent Component Analysis (ICA) or Principal Component Analysis (PCA). These algorithms mathematically isolate the variables that differ most significantly across the 16 spectral bands. Because the upper liturgical text and the erased lower text possess distinct chemical compositions, the algorithms successfully assign them to entirely separate output layers. We then map these isolated data layers to false-colour channels—rendering the erased script in vivid cyan against a dark background—creating a highly legible composite image without artificial hallucination or generative approximation.

Environmental Controls: 18°C & 50% RH Scanning Stage

Maintaining the structural integrity of ancient vellum during prolonged imaging sessions is our paramount concern. Fluctuating humidity rapidly alters the dimensional stability of animal skin, causing microscopic shrinkage that degrades image registration and permanently damages the artifact. To mitigate this risk, our imaging stage operates under an absolute, zero-tolerance environmental envelope, continuously monitoring the collagen shrinkage temperature throughout the session.

This strict climate isolation guarantees that the physical codex experiences zero thermal or hygroscopic stress, even during exhaustive 16-band scans. Archivists can confidently release their most fragile palimpsests knowing the physical substrate remains entirely uncompromised.

Chemical Metrology: Iron-Gall & Carbon Ink Spectroscopy

  • Iron-Gall Fluorescence Quenching: Historic iron-tannate inks absorb 365nm UV radiation, appearing as stark black voids against the naturally glowing keratin of the calfskin parchment.
  • Carbon Black Absorption: Standard soot-based inks remain opaque across the visible spectrum but become highly transparent in the upper Near-IR bands (1050nm), allowing us to "see through" heavy medieval redactions.
  • Vermilion Red Refraction: Cinnabar-based rubrics and illuminated initials exhibit unique spectral drop-offs between 550nm and 650nm, instantly distinguishing them from organic red dyes like madder or cochineal.
