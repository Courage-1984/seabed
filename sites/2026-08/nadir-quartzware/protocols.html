Standard Operating Procedure: Optical Decontamination

Following these step-by-step cleaning protocols prevents window etching and eliminates protein residue.

[ WARNING ] Avoid dichromate cleaning solutions entirely. Hexavalent chromium compounds will permanently bind to the synthetic quartz lattice, destroying UV transparency.
1. Solvent Rinse: Flush the interior cavity with Reagent-Grade Ethanol or Acetone to dissolve organic lipids.
2. Nitric Acid Bath: Submerge cuvette in a 10% (v/v) Nitric Acid solution and apply mild ultrasonic cleaning (max 35kHz) for 5 minutes.
3. Deionised Water Flush: Thoroughly rinse the interior and exterior using 18.2 MΩ·cm Type-1 ultra-pure water.
4. Nitrogen Gas Blow-Dry: Dry the optical windows using filtered, oil-free Nitrogen gas. Do not use wiping tissues.
Protocol 02: Blank Calibration & Autofluorescence Logging

Proper zero-referencing is critical for deep UV fluorescence profiling. To establish an instrument baseline, fill the cuvette with fresh, degassed Type-1 ultra-pure water. Ensure no micro-bubbles adhere to the optical windows.

Excite the blank sample using your instrument's primary UV laser (e.g., 266nm or 280nm) and monitor the water Raman peak (approx. 294nm to 300nm depending on excitation). The autofluorescence signal outside the Raman scatter should remain indistinguishable from the detector's dark noise.

Technical & Chemical Compatibility FAQ

Q: What is the maximum operating temperature for Suprasil cuvettes?

A: Suprasil 3001 can operate continuously at up to 1,000°C without devitrification. Ensure heating ramps do not exceed 200°C per hour to prevent thermal shock.

Q: Can Nadir cuvettes be autoclaved at 121°C?

A: Yes. Because our cells are molecularly fused without frits or adhesives, they withstand repeated 121°C / 15 psi steam autoclaving cycles indefinitely.

Q: How do I remove protein film buildup from micro-bore apertures?

A: We recommend a 30-minute soak in an enzymatic cleaner like Tergazyme, followed by standard acid washing and thorough ultrasonic deionized water flushing.

Q: What is the refractive index mismatch between seawater and fused silica?

A: The refractive index of Suprasil is approx 1.458 (at 589nm), while standard seawater is roughly 1.339. This difference must be accounted for in highly sensitive angular scattering configurations.

Q: Are optical windows resistant to hydrofluoric acid?

A: No. Hydrofluoric acid (HF) rapidly etches all forms of silica, including Suprasil quartz, permanently destroying the optical polish. Do not expose cuvettes to HF.
