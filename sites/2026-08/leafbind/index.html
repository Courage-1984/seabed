LeafBind operates a traditional bookbinding bench and handmade paper vat aboard the sailing barge SB Resolute in Tollesbury Fleet. 01 // THE FLOATING TIMBER BINDERY

We found the SB Resolute resting on the salt-marsh mudflats of Tollesbury Fleet after sixty years of hauling grain between Ipswich and London. Her pitch-pine hold offered three things no dry-land studio could match: ninety feet of clear timber floorboards, a stable ambient humidity that keeps damp rag paper from cockling as it dries, and the gentle tidal rise that lifts our hull twice every day.

Our papermaking vat sits directly over the barge keel, filled with fresh rainwater collected from our canvas deck awnings. We steep wild marsh reeds, unbleached flax, and cotton offcuts in heavy copper pots before processing them through our small water-driven Hollander beater. Each sheet is lifted by hand on woven brass wire moulds, pressed between woolen felts on an antique screw press, and hung from pine drying racks in the sea air.

In the aft cabin, under the warmth of a cast-iron stove, we fold, sew, and case every book by hand. We pare British vegetable-tanned calfskin on a flat marble slab, blind-stamp title lettering with hot brass type, and bind only two hundred finished volumes each year to ensure every hinge, endband, and deckled edge endures for generations.

The Floating Studio Disciplines

Patient Mastery Across the Book

01. LETTERPRESS FOLIO EDITIONS

Every letter is set by hand using cast metal type, meticulously locked into an iron chase, and printed slowly on our 1880 Albion iron hand press. The deep impression into our handmade deckled paper leaves a tactile, physical shadow that modern digital printing simply cannot replicate.

02. BOTANICAL PAPER VAT

Deep in the hold, our vat contains a living slurry of harvested marsh reeds and unbleached flax fibers. Through rhythmic, practiced motions, we form custom laid and wove sheets, pulling every individual piece by hand to create paper with unparalleled strength, longevity, and natural texture.

03. FULL-LEATHER BOOKBINDING

Our bindery sews every signature by hand onto raised hemp cords. We lace these directly into solid oak or millboard boards before covering the volume in British vegetable-tanned calfskin. The final touch is always a resilient, hand-worked silk endband to support the spine for centuries.

04. CONSERVATION RE-CASING

We provide sympathetic, structurally sound restoration for damaged family bibles, fragile 18th-century antiquarian folios, and ancient parchment manuscripts. We rely exclusively on reversible wheat starch paste, Japanese tissue repairs, and archival methods that honour the book's history.

Sourcing & Sustainability

Natural Chemistry & Local Harvest

Working respectfully with the estuarine landscape to source living materials that will endure the centuries.

Wild Reed Fibres

Each autumn, we wade into the tidal creeks to harvest Phragmites stems. Once retted in the salt water and beaten into pulp, these reeds yield incredibly strong, fibrous cellulose that gives our paper its distinctive pale moss hue.

Irish Flax & Linen Thread

We import unbleached long-staple flax to reinforce our paper vats, and we sew all bindings using heavily waxed Irish linen thread. This pure linen provides the archival tensile strength required to keep thick signatures securely bound over decades of continuous reading.

Organic Wheat Starch

Adhesives define a book's lifespan. We cook our own wheat starch paste fresh on the barge stove each morning. It is completely reversible, inherently archival, and guaranteed never to acid-burn or damage the precious paper it binds.
