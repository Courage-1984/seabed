The Art of the Papermaker

THE ESTUARY PAPER VAT

Making paper from the wild botanical landscape that surrounds our hull.

We practice the ancient, physically demanding craft of pulling individual sheets of paper from a vat of suspended botanical pulp. Far removed from industrial paper mills, our small floating studio relies entirely on harvested marsh reeds, rainwater, and physical labour to produce archival sheets that possess incredible structural integrity and tactile warmth.

The Sheet-Forming Cycle

From Marsh Grass to Archival Sheet

01. Fibre Retting

We harvest tough Phragmites reeds from the salt marshes at low tide. The stalks are bundled and submerged in tidal creeks for weeks, allowing natural enzymes to break down the non-cellulose materials. This process, known as retting, isolates the pure, strong botanical fibres required for durable paper.

02. Hollander Beating

The retted fibres and unbleached flax are fed into our Hollander beater—a heavy machine that macerates the plants in fresh water. Instead of cutting the fibres, the blunt blades fray and fibrillate them, creating microscopic tendrils that will eventually interlock to form an incredibly strong sheet of paper.

03. Deckle Dipping

The papermaker dips a wooden frame (the deckle) covered with woven brass wire into the vat of suspended pulp. With a smooth, practiced shake, the water drains through the wire mesh while the fibres settle and lock together on the surface, forming the distinctive raw deckled edge of a handmade sheet.

04. Felt Pressing

Each wet, fragile sheet is couched (transferred) onto a woven woolen felt. We build a stack of alternating felts and paper, then place the entire post into our antique cast-iron screw press. Tremendous pressure forces out the remaining water, compressing the fibres into a solid, cohesive sheet ready for the drying racks.

Material Dossier

Cellulose & Archival Longevity

The permanence of a book relies entirely on the chemical stability of its paper. Commercial paper is often heavily bleached and sized with acidic rosins that cause it to yellow, embrittle, and disintegrate within a few decades.

Cellulose Purity

By using wild botanicals and linen rag instead of cheap wood pulp, our paper retains incredibly long cellulose chains. This length provides the mechanical folding endurance necessary for a book page that will be turned thousands of times over centuries.

Neutral pH Sizing

To prevent ink from feathering, paper must be sized. We reject acidic chemical sizes entirely, opting instead for a traditional, pH-neutral cellulose size applied internally to the vat. This ensures the paper remains chemically inert and safe for historic documents.

Lightfastness

Because we never use optical brighteners or harsh chemical bleaches, our paper maintains its natural, warm oat hue indefinitely. Laboratory accelerated ageing tests verify our sheets resist UV degradation, preserving the contrast of letterpress ink.

Studio Inquiries

Papermaking FAQ

Does the paper feather with calligraphy ink?

No. We size all our sheets internally with a neutral cellulose size, and surface-size our 180 GSM flax sheets with a traditional gelatin wash. This creates a resilient barrier that holds sharp, crisp ink lines without bleeding or feathering.

Can you create custom deckle watermarks?

Yes. We can twist fine brass wire onto our wooden deckle moulds to form custom monograms or estate crests. When the paper is pulled, the wire displaces the pulp, leaving a permanent, translucent watermark in every bespoke sheet.

Why does the weight of handmade paper vary?

Unlike industrial machines, a human papermaker relies entirely on muscle memory and the viscosity of the vat. While we aim for standard GSM weights, a natural variation of 10-15% between individual sheets is the true hallmark of authentic hand-pulled paper.

Do you offer weekend papermaking workshops?

During the summer months, we host intimate two-day workshops aboard the barge in Tollesbury Fleet. Participants learn to harvest reeds, operate the Hollander beater, and pull their own deckled sheets over the vat. Space is strictly limited to four students.

How do you safely ship delicate deckled sheets?

We pack all loose paper orders completely flat between heavy millboard and ship them in rigid, weather-resistant archival sleeves. We never roll our handmade paper, as doing so can permanently damage the crisp deckled edges and crack the heavy sizing.

How should I care for raw botanical paper?

Our paper is inherently archival, but it remains a living material that responds to its environment. We recommend storing loose sheets flat in a dry, dark archival box to prevent the natural botanical fibres from expanding in high humidity.
