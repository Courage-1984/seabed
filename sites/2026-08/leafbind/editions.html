Structural Mechanics

Historical Binding Styles

Every structure offers a distinct mechanical advantage and reading experience.

Exposed Coptic Stitch

A structure dating back to early Egyptian codices, the Coptic binding features completely exposed chain stitching across the spine. Its greatest mechanical virtue is the ability to lay entirely flat on a table, making it an exceptional structure for sketchbooks, ledgers, and art portfolios.

Full Goat-Skin over Oak

The quintessential archival structure. Signatures are sewn onto heavy raised cords, which are then laced tightly into solid oak boards. Covered in durable goat-skin leather and finished with hand-woven endbands, this style provides unparalleled protection for heavy, permanent library volumes.

Japanese Stab Binding

A refined, elegant structure perfect for binding single loose sheets or delicate prints. The thread passes completely through the block of pages near the spine edge in decorative geometric patterns. It provides a firm, rigid spine ideal for slim poetry chapbooks and exhibition catalogues.
