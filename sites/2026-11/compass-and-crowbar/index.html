SECTION 02 // DISCIPLINARY HYBRIDISATION // JOINT FIELD OPERATION

AN UNHOLY ALLIANCE OF ARTISAN PRINTMAKERS AND WRECKING CREWS

The commercial art world prefers its heritage safely miniaturised, framed behind glare-free glass, and politely illuminated in whisper-quiet gallery rooms. We find that attitude entirely insufficient for a three-thousand-tonne grain elevator or a riveted steel gantry crane. When a municipal council condemns a monumental mid-century facility, standard conservation groups arrive with clipboards and petitions that are invariably ignored. Compass & Crowbar approaches the demolition contractor instead, trading technical site safety compliance, night-shift logistics, and a share of art print proceeds for exclusive rigging rights on the scaffolding.

The arrangement is delightfully pragmatic. The demolition crew provides the mobile telehandlers, four-point rigging harnesses, and industrial water tankers; our printmakers bring fifty-litre carboys of potassium ferricyanide and bolts of unbleached Belgian linen spanning the full height of the façade. As the demolition excavators idle during their mid-day break, we unroll the photosensitive fabric across the building’s skeletal trusses, letting four hundred square metres of chemical-soaked textile bake under natural ultraviolet radiation. When the exposure reaches peak bronze-green saturation, the crew fires up the firehoses to wash the unexposed salts straight into our mobile neutralizing runoff tanks, fixing an indelible Prussian-blue shadow of structural steel hours before it is cut into scrap.

PHASE 01 // LIQUID SENSITISATION 03:00 — 05:30 UTC // NOCTURNAL WINDOW

Nocturnal Embrocation Under Amber Floodlights

Field production begins in total pitch darkness four hours before dawn. Inside our sealed sea-container chemical mixing skids, technicians blend fifty-kilogram batches of ferric ammonium citrate and potassium ferricyanide in deionised water at twelve degrees Celsius. Working beneath low-pressure sodium safety lamps, printmakers use modified agricultural airless paint sprayers to flood four hundred square metres of raw Belgian unbleached linen with the green-gold sensitising emulsion.

The coated fabric is drawn through dual vulcanised rubber squeegee drums to ensure uniform fibre absorption without saturation pooling. High-velocity centrifugal air driers cure the damp textile to a tack-free state within sixty minutes, after which the immense fabric web is wound onto steel core spools and wrapped in light-impermeable tarpaulins ready for crane ascension.

PHASE 02 // ACTINIC EXPOSURE 11:30 — 13:45 UTC // MIDDAY ELEVATION

Solar Bake & Structural Shadow Masking

As the demolition contractors shut down heavy machinery for their mid-day break, our high-angle rigging specialists ascend the scaffolding towers. Using electric hoist winches and four-point harness tethers, the forty-metre roll of sensitized linen is unrolled vertically down the condemned elevation. Marine-grade 316 stainless steel eyelet grommets are anchored every four hundred millimetres to perimeter wire ropes tensioned to twelve kilonewtons, keeping the cloth immaculately flat against the structural steel.

The doomed building itself serves as the photographic negative. Riveted I-beams, cross-braced lattice trusses, external fire escapes, and gantry rails cast razor-sharp shadows across the reactive surface. A pyranometer station measures real-time solar irradiance, calculating cloud attenuation and the sun’s azimuth shift to ensure crisp exposure definition before penumbral blurring occurs.

PHASE 03 // CHEMICAL QUENCH 14:00 — 15:30 UTC // HYDRAULIC DELUGE

The Firehose Deluge & Closed-Loop Quench

When the exposed linen reaches peak bronze-green saturation, development must be halted instantaneously to stop photochemical solarization. The site demolition crew connects two high-pressure firehoses to municipal river hydrants, drenching the full forty-metre textile in fifteen thousand litres of pressurized water at eight bar.

Within seconds, unexposed yellow-green iron salts wash away into perimeter collection troughs, revealing the insoluble, permanent Prussian-blue ferric ferrocyanide molecular lattice beneath. Industrial carpet extraction wands remove excess water from the hanging fabric before wind gusts can cause textile distortion, while subterranean slurry pumps direct all chemical effluent into our mobile multi-stage neutralization skids.

SECTION 08 // TECHNICAL ACCORDION // CURATORIAL SPECIFICATIONS

LOGISTICAL & CURATORIAL INQUIRIES

Practical answers regarding the suspension engineering, photochemistry, and site legalities of monumental 30-metre industrial textiles.

How do you hang a 30-metre textile in an indoor gallery or museum atrium?

Monumental textiles are suspended via custom continuous anodised aluminium aircraft-cable battens clamped through reinforced 316-grade brass perimeter grommets spaced at 400mm centres. Turnbuckles distribute tension across primary structural roof trusses, allowing the textile to hang flat without localized scalloping or fabric distortion. Weight load is approximately 65kg per forty-metre banner.

Will the cyanotype fade under standard museum exhibition lighting?

Natural Prussian blue (ferric ferrocyanide) is remarkably lightfast and chemically stable. Unlike fugitive organic dye prints, temporary photo-bleaching under prolonged artificial gallery lighting is completely reversible: resting the textile in total darkness or brief atmospheric humidity regenerates oxidized color saturation through spontaneous atmospheric oxidation.

How do you secure legal access and safety compliance on hazardous demolition sites?

Compass & Crowbar operates as an integrated specialist subcontractor under the Construction (Design and Management) Regulations 2015. All field printmakers hold CPCS slinger/signaller and advanced working-at-height licenses, carry £10m commercial contractor insurance, and maintain site-specific Method Statements and Risk Assessments approved by principal site contractors.

What happens if a rain squall or coastal mist hits during the solar bake?

Precipitation initiates immediate localized washdown streaks across unexposed iron salts. We never discard weather-marked exposures; localized water droplet blooms, rain-wash curtains, and atmospheric mist artifacts are catalogued as authentic meteorological data of the demolition day, recording the climate of the structure's final hours.

Can private collectors or municipal libraries purchase smaller sections?

While our primary output consists of continuous full-elevation works for civic spaces, we periodically release 1:1 architectural detail cuts—individual photograms of riveted gusset plates, valve wheels, and structural anchor pins—mounted on archival honeycomb aluminium stretchers (1.5m × 1.5m) suited for standard residential or corporate interiors.

How quickly can your field unit mobilize to an emergency structural demolition?

We maintain two containerized mobile darkroom skids equipped with chemical reserves, linen rolls, and pressurized washing equipment. For emergency municipal demolitions across the UK mainland, our survey crew can mobilize and rig within 48 hours of site notice, coordinating directly with site plant operators.
