Seasonal Saltmarsh Dispatches

Every PegLow pattern modification is evaluated across three full tide cycles before we cut production cloth. Here are unedited entries transcribed directly from our waterproof field notebooks.

14 OCTOBER 2026 CLEY MARSHES, NORTH NORFOLK WIND: WNW FORCE 7 · SLEET SQUALLS

Six-Hour Tide Vigil: Reedbed Single-Pole Hide

Arrived on the seawall track at 05:15 in pitch black. Set the windward stakes into soft brackish peat using boot heel pressure; no mallet strike to disturb sleeping waders on the scrape. The single carbon mast snapped into the central grommet in forty seconds flat.

By 07:30, a squall of sleet was driving horizontally off the Wash. Ultralight nylon hides would have flapped like loose spinnakers; the 180 gsm waxed cotton draped silently across its tension lines. A juvenile bittern emerged from the reeds twenty paces away and hunted sticklebacks in the ditch for forty-five minutes, completely oblivious to our presence inside the shelter.

Equipment Notes: Horn toggles operated cleanly with neoprene gloves; zero water seepage through the tripod collar seam.

28 JULY 2026 WICKEN FEN, CAMBRIDGESHIRE AMBIENT TEMP: 14°C · STEADY SUMMER DRIZZLE

Overnight Moth Recording: Moth-Trap Rain Canopy

Deployed two Robinson mercury-vapour traps along the sedge fen boardwalk. Rain started at 23:45 and continued uninterrupted until 04:30. In previous years, plastic tarpaulins created warm micro-climates that trapped moisture inside the trap bucket and shattered hot mercury bulbs.

The PegLow canopy’s breathable weave permitted convection airflow to escape upward while directing water outside the flight cone. Recorded 142 species by sunrise, including five Cream-bordered Green Peas and an immaculate Leopard Moth. Not a single bulb failure.

Equipment Notes: Brass corner eyelets tied off cleanly to alder branches using natural jute cords.

04 FEBRUARY 2026 RSPB MINSMERE, SUFFOLK TIDE RISING · GROUND FROST -3°C

Dawn Frozen Mud Trial: Closed-Cell Bog Kneeler

Stationary low-angle observation of feeding bearded tits in frozen reed stubble. Standard rubber kneeling pads conduct cold directly from frozen ground into knee joints within twenty minutes, prompting involuntary fidgeting.

The thirty-millimetre nitrogen-blown closed-cell core maintained total thermal isolation for three and a half hours on ice-crusted mud. Sharp broken reed culms failed to pierce the heavy waxed-cotton envelope.

Equipment Notes: Wiped clean with a handful of saltmarsh grass back at the vehicle. No mud penetration.

MAINTENANCE & FIELD QUESTIONS

Operational Questions & Practical Answers

We do not employ marketing bot scripts. These are concise, factual answers to the questions naturalists ask us most frequently at bird fairs and field stations.

How often does Halley Stevensons dry-waxed ripstop require re-proofing?
For an observer using their shelter twenty weekends a year in typical UK coastal weather, a full re-waxing is needed only every two to three years. The dry-wax paraffin formulation does not wash away in rain like oilskin dressings. We supply a £12 Scottish paraffin-and-beeswax bar that can be rubbed directly over high-wear fold seams and gently warmed with a domestic hair dryer in ten minutes.
Why do you refuse to use silent rubberised zippers or magnetic closures?
Fine Norfolk coastal silt and salt sand particles foul zipper tracks within hours, causing coils to split or jam permanently when yanked. Magnetic closures attract black iron-sand particles found along North Sea beaches, clogging mating surfaces. Turned horn toggles and braided cord loops contain zero moving parts, cannot jam with mud, and will outlive the owner.
Can replacement Easton carbon mast sections be ordered if accidentally trodden on?
Yes. We stock individual Easton carbon mast segments and machined marine-brass ferrules at our Wells workshop. If an errant cow steps on your mast during a marsh survey, we dispatch individual replacement sections via first class post for £18, saving you from purchasing a whole new pole assembly.
What are the exact dimensions of the silent spotting-scope viewing port?
The front observation aperture measures 28 cm wide by 34 cm high when fully unlatched. It accommodates large objective spotting scopes up to 95 mm (including Swarovski ATX and Leica Televid systems) as well as 600 mm prime wildlife photography lenses on gimbal heads, while the pleated cotton skirt seals tightly around tripod legs below.
Do you ship to European bird observatories and ringers outside the UK?
We ship regularly via tracked international post to naturalists in the Netherlands, Scandinavia, Germany, and France. European shipments include duty-prepaid documentation so field researchers do not experience customs hold-ups. We also offer direct bulk invoicing for university research teams and British Trust for Ornithology (BTO) ringing groups.
