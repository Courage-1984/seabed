Autonomous Tether-Drop Logistics

Landing a heavy-lift drone in an active, dusty open-pit mine poses severe safety risks. To bypass this, the Kestrel fleet utilises a proprietary tether-drop system. Once our aircraft arrives at the designated GPS coordinates, it hovers at an altitude of forty metres, well above ground-level obstacles, active machinery, and dust clouds.

The payload—secured in a reinforced, shock-absorbent synthetic crate—is lowered to the surface via a high-tensile motorised winch. Onboard LIDAR sensors monitor the descent rate, automatically braking as the crate makes contact with the ground. Once slack is detected in the line, the mechanical release hook disengages autonomously, and the aircraft immediately ascends to return to the Inverness hub. The entire deployment sequence takes under two minutes. Your site engineers recover the part and resume operations immediately, entirely bypassing the fragile last mile of rural road infrastructure.

Drop Zone Authorisation

Our tether-drop protocol is strictly procedural and highly efficient. To initiate an autonomous delivery, a site manager must complete three mandatory steps to ensure absolute safety and logistical precision.

  1. Submit Coordinates: Enter precise GPS coordinates via the secure Kestrel procurement portal. The system instantly verifies flight viability based on airspace restrictions and the operational radius from our Inverness hub.
  2. Clear the Zone: Establish a designated 10x10 metre drop zone. This area must be completely free of overhead powerlines, towering cranes, and active heavy traffic to ensure an unobstructed descent path for the payload winch.
  3. Monitor Telemetry: Receive automated telemetry alerts directly to your mobile device, providing real-time flight tracking, payload status, and an estimated time of arrival accurate down to the minute.

Failure to clear the drop zone will trigger an automatic abort sequence, and the aircraft will return to base with the components.

Flight & Payload Tolerances

Our fleet comprises bespoke multi-rotor aircraft engineered for heavy lift in unforgiving climates. We operate strictly within established aviation tolerances to guarantee safe and successful payload delivery. The maximum slung payload capacity is rigidly capped at 250kg per flight. We maintain a maximum operational radius of 120 kilometres from our Inverness dispatch hub, ensuring sufficient battery reserves for the return journey.

While our drones are heavily weatherised, absolute safety remains paramount. Sustained winds exceeding 35 knots, or severe atmospheric icing conditions, will temporarily ground all flights until the weather window clears. We do not compromise on these operational limits, as preserving the integrity of both the aircraft and your essential parts dictates our dispatch procedures.

Site managers must factor these constraints into their emergency planning, particularly during the harsh Scottish winter months.
