SECTION 01 // CIVIC ASSET SHIELD

The SurgeCap Mk-IV Modular Bulkhead

During a severe North Sea surge event, water levels in estuarine channels can rise by more than three metres in less than two hours. When this hydrostatic head presses against low-lying industrial perimeters, unsealed silo bases and aeration shafts experience upward water hammer that lifts standard timber covers and disintegrates sandbag walls. The SurgeCap Mk-IV was engineered specifically for rapid crane placement directly onto out-of-round masonry and corroded steel copings, utilizing three segmented arc plates of cast marine-grade 6082-T6 aluminium that interlock over a central expansion hub.

Once lowered into the shaft collar by a standard mobile boom crane, our two-man rigging team actuates four perimeter turnbuckles that drive radial expansion wedges outward against the shaft walls. A dual-lip cellular EPDM seal ring deforms into surface pitting, brick spalls, and uneven mortar lines, establishing a positive hydraulic barrier capable of withstanding 4.2 bar of reverse hydrostatic pressure. The entire installation requires zero through-bolting or chemical anchors into fragile aged brickwork, allowing the assembly to lock securely in under ninety minutes while remaining completely removable once the spring tide recedes.

Every unit fabricated in our Norfolk yard undergoes rigorous hydrostatic pressure certification on our calibrated test rig before delivery to municipal depot reserves. Integral two-inch non-return venting valves allow trapped internal air to evacuate smoothly during rapid external inundation, preventing explosive pneumatic pressure spikes beneath the seal. Where municipal authorities once faced permanent shaft filling or catastrophic subterranean flooding, the Mk-IV provides an immediate, deployable shield that protects municipal foundations across decades of unpredictable coastal weather.

SECTION 02 // MECHANICAL SYSTEM DESIGN

Hydrostatic Resistance & Structural Anatomy

Municipal flood barriers cannot rely on gravity alone when subterranean voids become pressurized conduits. In an estuarine surge, backflow pressure accumulates beneath unvented shaft lids at accelerating velocities. The SurgeCap architecture converts incoming hydraulic thrust into outward radial locking force.

The mechanical assembly anchors three interlocking kinetic sub-systems designed to absorb multi-ton dynamic hydraulic shocks while preserving the fragile masonry of Victorian brick culverts and weathered 1960s concrete collars:

02.A

Articulated Shear Pins (240 kN)

Forged from passivated 316L marine stainless steel, these central shear keys transfer lateral tidal thrust directly into the outer segmented ring structure. Each pin incorporates a sacrificial brass shear indicator that validates load distribution across all three arc sectors without compromising the primary hydraulic seal.

RATED TOLERANCE: 240 kN DYNAMIC IMPACT LOAD
02.B

Telescopic Torque Struts (8% Ovality)

Centuries of soil subsidence and tidal scouring leave municipal caissons deformed from true circularity. Our threaded turnbuckle struts feature differential spherical bearings that expand eccentrically, accommodating up to 8% orifice ovality without jamming or creating uneven perimeter gasket gaps.

COMPENSATION: UP TO 280mm CROSS-AXIS DEVIATION
02.C

Bi-Directional Equalisation Manifold

Equipped with twin two-inch bronze poppet check valves, this manifold allows high-volume trapped air displacement during initial inundation, while snapping shut in 0.08 seconds once water hits the lower baffle plate, blocking reverse tidal back-flow entirely.

ACTIVATION SPEED: < 80 MILLISECONDS DUAL-SEAL

Self-Energising Hydraulic Wedge Principle

Unlike traditional counter-weighted hatch covers that weaken as water levels rise, the SurgeCap's 14-degree Cam-Lok wedge geometry utilizes external hydrostatic head to drive the seal tighter. As estuarine water climbs over the cap, downward head pressure acts on the dished aluminium deck, forcing the radial expansion shoes deeper into the shaft wall. The higher the tidal surge, the tighter the mechanical grip.

SECTION 07 // CIVIC ENGINEERING INQUIRIES

Civic Engineering & Deployment Inquiries

Practical answers for drainage board superintendents, municipal structural consultants, and port harbour engineers planning emergency coastal asset protection.

01 // Can the SurgeCap be installed without a heavy all-terrain crane on cramped quaysides? +

Yes. While mobile boom cranes provide the fastest deployment, each arc segment of our Series A and B kits is fitted with balanced lifting eyes suitable for compact Hiab lorry-mounted knuckle cranes or portable aluminium gantry frames. Because each individual segment weighs under 230 kilograms, two riggers can assemble and lock the bulkhead in confined dockside alleys where large cranes cannot establish outriggers.

02 // How does the system ensure a watertight seal against severely pitted 19th-century brickwork? +

Our dual-lip EPDM collar incorporates a deep 45mm deformable cellular bulb designed specifically for historic masonry. When perimeter torque wedges are tightened to 450 Nm, the pliable elastomer flows into pitted brick surfaces, washed-out mortar beds, and minor step fractures. For severely eroded shafts with missing brick courses, we provide an optional pre-cast annular levelling collar that seats in ten minutes without cement curing.

03 // What prevents dangerous air pressure buildup beneath the cap as rising tides flood lower conduits? +

Every SurgeCap features twin integral 2-inch bronze non-return equalization valves fitted with calibrated flotation balls. As floodwater enters the lower subterranean pipework, trapped air exhausts freely through the valves at velocities up to 800 litres per minute. The moment rising water reaches the underside of the bulkhead plate, internal buoyant valves seal instantaneously, preventing water hammer and pneumatic blowouts.

04 // Does deploying the cap require personnel to enter the confined space of the shaft? +

Under no circumstances. SiloShield engineering strictly prohibits confined space entry during emergency deployment. All hoist rigging, arc plate alignment, central shear-key insertion, and wedge torque actuation are performed entirely from ground-level quayside decking using extended torque ratchets. Crew members operate safely behind standard edge-protection barriers throughout the procedure.

05 // What maintenance is required for municipal depots keeping caps on standby storage? +

Depot storage requirements are minimal. Modular aluminium plates are stacked horizontally in galvanised steel storage cradles supplied with each kit. We recommend an annual visual inspection of the turnbuckle threads and a light silicone spray application to the EPDM gaskets. SiloShield technicians provide free annual depot audits for all accredited UK drainage board inventories.

06 // Are SiloShield assemblies approved under UK Environment Agency flood asset registers? +

Yes. SiloShield capping heads are fully certified under BS EN 124 load ratings and meet Environment Agency requirements for Class 1 Demountable Flood Defenses. Full technical calculation dossiers, finite element analysis (FEA) reports, and hydraulic test rig certification are archived under municipal asset register references for rapid insurance compliance.
