Dossier Ref 1941-MACF

SS Clan MacFarlane — Low-Pressure Cylinder

During winter Atlantic convoy service, the 6,200 GRT cargo steamer SS Clan MacFarlane suffered catastrophic cylinder wall cleavage when sub-zero condensate water-hammer ruptured the 8-ton low-pressure steam cylinder casting. Admiralty surveyors initially condemned the engine block as a total constructive loss.

Our Govan mobile squad boarded the vessel at Basin Slip 3, establishing round-the-clock pneumatic shifts. Working in freezing conditions inside the engine room, engineers laced a 1.4-metre bifurcated crack through the 75mm cast-iron chest with 142 Invar keys and 310 cold studs, returning the ship to convoy duty in 96 hours. The casting remained in active service until vessel scrapping in 1974.

  • Vessel & Tonnage:SS Clan MacFarlane (6,200 GRT Cargo Vessel)
  • Component:8-Tonne Triple Expansion LP Steam Cylinder
  • Fracture Seam:1.4m Bifurcated Crack Through 75mm Cast Wall
  • Subsequent Service:33 Years Continuous Sea Operations
Dossier Ref 1962-CLYD7

Clyde Dredger No. 7 — Bilge & Spoil Volute

Clyde Port Authority bucket dredger No. 7 suffered a massive internal blowout when a submerged granite boulder wedged inside its 12-tonne centrifugal spoil pump housing. The resulting 1.8-metre circumferential fracture breached the 90mm cast-iron casing, threatening the vessel with immediate sinking.

In-situ dry-dock staging allowed engineers to relieve internal suction line strain before jig-slotting 220 high-nickel Invar keys across the spiral chamber. Interlocking copper-nickel stud laces sealed the casing against 28 bar surge pressure, avoiding a six-month foundry re-casting shutdown.

  • Vessel & Tonnage:Clyde Dredger No. 7 (Clyde Port Authority)
  • Component:12-Tonne Centrifugal Spoil Pump Volute
  • Fracture Seam:1.8m Circumferential Split Across 90mm Shell
  • Subsequent Service:24 Years Dredging the River Clyde Channel
Dossier Ref 1988-IONA

MV Iona — Twin-Screw Steering Engine

En route through the Minch in heavy gale conditions, the Caledonian MacBrayne passenger car ferry MV Iona experienced hydraulic pressure spike failure in its heavy cast-iron steering engine casing. The steering tiller arm fractured the hydraulic cylinder trunnion web clean off the base casting.

Mobilising to Gourock within three hours, Govan technicians worked through the night to precision-mill multi-lobed transverse keyways across the sheared trunnion interface. Using high-tensile Invar keys with work-hardened pre-stressing, the steering casing was certified by Lloyd’s Register in time for the morning tidal crossing.

  • Vessel & Tonnage:MV Iona (CalMac Passenger & Vehicle Ferry)
  • Component:Hydraulic Steering Engine Trunnion Casing
  • Fracture Seam:Complete Sheared Flange Web Over 650mm Seam
  • Turnaround Time:28 Hours From Berthing to Passenger Sign-Off
Dossier Ref 2024-AILSA

Trawler Ailsa Craig — Propulsion Bedplate

Operating in rough North Atlantic waters off Rockall, the deep-sea freezer trawler Ailsa Craig suffered severe main engine foundation bolt failure, resulting in an 850mm vertical fracture cleaving through the primary aft main bearing pocket of the cast-iron engine bedplate.

Towed to the Govan Graving Docks, the engine bedplate was leveled on precision optical transits. Forty-eight heavy Invar keys were pneumatic-peened into the bearing saddle web while cold stud lacing restored oil-tight integrity. Laser alignment verified bearing bore concentricity within 0.015 mm, saving the vessel owners £380,000 in engine replacement costs.

  • Vessel & Tonnage:FV Ailsa Craig (1,150 GRT Ocean Freezer Trawler)
  • Component:Six-Cylinder Diesel Propulsion Bedplate
  • Fracture Seam:850mm Vertical Crack Through Bearing Saddle
  • Alignment Verification:0.015 mm Datum Tolerance Across Bores

Technical Inquiries · Section V

Technical & Classification Enquiries FAQ

Precise engineering answers to common operational, regulatory, and mechanical questions regarding zero-heat cold-stitching of marine machinery.

1. Can cold-stitching be carried out while a vessel remains afloat at anchor?

Yes. Because the Govan cold-stitching system relies exclusively on portable magnetic-base pneumatic tools with zero flame, welding arc, or hot-work fire hazards, repairs are frequently performed inside engine rooms while vessels lie at anchor or alongside commercial discharging berths. Classification society surveyors regularly witness and certify in-situ hydrostatic proofing without requiring dry-dock diversion.

2. What is the maximum wall thickness of cast iron that can be structurally laced?

Our standard multi-lobe keyway profiles are calibrated for wall thicknesses ranging from 12mm up to 250mm in monolithic grey iron. In heavy turbine casings and massive hydraulic press platens exceeding 150mm wall section, keys are peened in staggered depth tiers from both sides of the casting, ensuring full cross-sectional tensile load absorption across the entire fracture plane.

3. How do classification societies certify cold-stitched repairs for unrestricted ocean-going service?

Classification societies including Lloyd’s Register, DNV, and Bureau Veritas recognize mechanical cold-stitching under standard machinery repair rules. Certification requires preliminary magnetic particle crack arrest verification, metallurgical alloy tracing, and an attended 48-hour hydrostatic hold test at 1.5 times operating pressure, following which unrestricted vessel survey notation is formally granted.

4. Can cold-stitching seal fractures in internal combustion engine water jackets and liners?

Yes. The overlapping stud lacing process creates an impervious metal-to-metal interference seal capable of containing pressurized cooling water, lubricating oil, and combustion gases. By cold-working ductile copper-nickel studs directly into the tapped threads between Invar keys, the repair withstands cyclic engine vibration and coolant temperatures up to 130°C without elastomer adhesives.

5. What guarantees are provided regarding joint integrity and pressure tightness?

Every marine casting repaired by Govan Cold-Stitch Marine Salvage carries a permanent registration certificate in the Clyde Marine Historical Casting Register, guaranteeing structural shear resistance up to 380 N/mm² and complete freedom from thermal distortion for the remaining operating life of the machinery under normal classification operating parameters.
