SEC 02 // CIVIL HYDROLOGY

Sub-Shingle Hydraulic Stabilization

STATUTORY SPECIFICATION BRITISH STANDARD BS EN 1992-3
HYDRAULIC GRADIENT ARREST DOCTRINE // REPORT REF: CT-HYD-1961 ACTIVE MANDATE

The Dungeness cuspate foreland constitutes an unstable geological mass of over twelve billion tonnes of flint pebbles overlying permeable sand and silty clay. Rainwater filtering through this vast gravel sponge gathers in a lens of fresh groundwater resting directly atop dense, encroaching seawater. During spring tides combined with south-westerly gales, hydraulic head pressure from the Channel forces saltwater through deep permeable strata, threatening to breach coastal utility basements and contaminate municipal boreholes across Romney Marsh.

Culvertrak’s sub-surface galleries establish an artificial hydraulic gradient that arrests saline migration at the shoreline perimeter. Precast concrete culvert pipes up to 3.6 metres in diameter are driven horizontally through the gravel bed using hydraulic thrust boring, forming continuous drainage conduits with invert levels set precisely four metres below Ordnance Datum Newlyn. Self-scouring flume profiles maintain minimum self-cleaning velocities of 1.2 metres per second even during low-flow summer intervals, preventing fine marine silt from choking the outfall valves. When sea levels exceed the coastal drainage threshold, automated counter-weighted flap gates seal within three seconds, redirecting drainage discharge into high-capacity dewatering sumps for pressurized offshore release.

SEC 05 // DEFENSE READINESS

High-Capacity Storm Surge Sluice Deployment

SURGE DEPLOYMENT 12,000 L/s RATED PUMP RELIEF

When severe gale-force spring tides raise Channel sea levels above the gravity discharge threshold, gravity outfalls seal against backflow. Emergency dewatering systems deploy twin high-capacity axial-flow flood pumps to prevent backpressure inundation across Romney Marsh.

EMERGENCY BYPASS PROTOCOL // DIRECTIVE CT-DP-08 MAX SURGE DISCHARGE: 12,000 L/s

Axial-Flow Dewatering Assembly

The Outfall 04 emergency dewatering station is equipped with twin 450-kilowatt vertical axial-flow flood pumps mounted directly within a six-metre-deep reinforced concrete sump chamber. When tidal flaps lock shut, storm run-off accumulates in the retention manifold. Once piezometric head sensors register +1.20 metres ODN, automated variable-speed drives engage pump impeller bank A within twelve seconds. If surge levels persist above +1.50 metres ODN, pump bank B triggers concurrently to achieve the full twelve thousand litres per second discharge rate into the English Channel.
